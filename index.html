03 / The reason

What was driving it?

Not one shock, but three overlapping ones. PCA names the shape; the news flow explains why it happened.

01

A flight to quality

The failure of SVB and Signature sent deposits and risk capital toward government-backed assets. Treasury buying pushed yields down across the curve — a large negative PC1.

PC1 · level
02

The Fed path repriced

The 2Y is the market’s policy-rate proxy. As investors moved from “higher for longer” to “hikes near the end, cuts later,” the front end fell far more than the long end. That is a positive PC2.

PC2 · slope
03

Global bank stress

Credit Suisse’s crisis and the SNB backstop globalised the fear. The long end stayed relatively anchored: the 30Y fell only 28 bp, because inflation and fiscal risk did not disappear.

PC2 + PC3
04

A twist, not a wave

The 5Y underperformed the 2Y but outperformed the 10Y. The 3-factor model captures the broad shape, but leaves a 9.5 bp residual at the 2Y — a useful reminder that three factors are a summary.

PC3 · curvature

PCA is descriptive, not causal. The labels “level”, “slope” and “curvature” are interpretations of the eigenvectors, not identified macroeconomic shocks.

04 / The method

How the toy works.

The numbers are estimated from daily changes in US Treasury par yields. The web toy uses the same loadings, base curve and event data as the report.

The model
Δy(τ) ≈ s₁ · PC1(τ) + s₂ · PC2(τ) + s₃ · PC3(τ)

The curve on the screen is y(τ) = y₀(τ) + Σ sⱼ · PCⱼ(τ), where y₀ is 9 March 2023 and the sliders are sⱼ in units of the daily standard deviation of each factor. The loadings are unit vectors from the covariance matrix of daily yield changes, so the three factors are orthogonal by construction.

  • Sample: 4 Oct 1993 – 8 Mar 2023; 6,364 daily changes. The 20Y and 30Y tenors are included, so the sample starts when the 20Y becomes available and skips the 2002–06 30Y discontinuation.
  • Tenors: 3M, 6M, 1Y, 2Y, 3Y, 5Y, 7Y, 10Y, 20Y, 30Y. Par yields, in percent, from the US Treasury.
  • Estimation: covariance PCA on first differences; no standardisation, because all tenors are in the same units. Signs are chosen so PC1 is a level shift, PC2 a steepener and PC3 a wings-up twist.
  • Event: the 9→17 March change is projected onto the pre-event loadings. The 3-factor fit explains 95.4% of the cross-section (R² around the mean); the residual is largest at the 2Y.

Why 96% is enough — and not enough.

Three factors capture almost all of the variance, but the March 2023 move was so large and front-end-heavy that the 3-factor fit misses the 2Y by 9.5 bp. The toy is a lens, not a trading model.
